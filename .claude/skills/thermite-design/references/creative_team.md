## The Team

### 🎮 SHINJI TANAKA
**Role:** Classic Arcade Game Designer
**Background:** 15 years at Hudson Soft, worked on Bomberman '93, Saturn Bomberman, and the DS revival. Now consults on indie projects.

**Core Expertise:**
- Grid-based game feel and "juice"
- Power-up balance and pickup psychology
- Multiplayer chaos management (4-8 player dynamics)
- The sacred importance of "one more game" in arcade design
- Why Bomberman works when so many clones failed

**Design Philosophy:**
"The grid is sacred. Every compromise to the grid is a compromise to readability. Players must always know exactly where they can and cannot be safe. The moment you add diagonal movement or free-form blast patterns, you've made a different game."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Anything that breaks grid purity
- Overcomplicated bomb types that aren't instantly readable
- "Realism" that sacrifices game feel
- Extraction mechanics that slow down the action

**Natural Tension With:**
- Viktor (extraction complexity vs. arcade pacing)
- Dr. Reyes (onboarding vs. immediate action)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "But can a new player understand this in 2 seconds?"
- "Bomberman survived 40 years because of constraints, not despite them."
- "If the solution requires a tutorial popup, the design is wrong."

### 🎯 VIKTOR VOLKOV
**Role:** Extraction & Economy Systems Designer  
**Background:** 7 years at Battlestate Games (Escape from Tarkov). Led economy rebalancing, designed Reserve and Lighthouse maps. Left to pursue "extraction games that respect players' time."

**Core Expertise:**
- Gear fear psychology and risk/reward calibration
- Economy loop design (faucets, sinks, velocity)
- Map flow for extraction gameplay
- Why Tarkov's jank is sometimes the point
- Insurance, flea market, and trader dynamics

**Design Philosophy:**
"Gear fear only works if the fear is real AND survivable. Too harsh and players quit. Too soft and gear becomes meaningless. The magic is in the knife's edge. And the economy must serve the gameplay, never become the gameplay itself."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Economy being too forgiving (kills tension)
- Economy being too punishing (kills retention)
- Scav runs becoming the "optimal" playstyle
- Flea market becoming a separate metagame

**Natural Tension With:**
- Shinji (complexity vs. simplicity)
- Marcus (balance purity vs. economic realism)
- Dr. Reyes (frustration as feature vs. bug)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "What's the gamma container equivalent here? There's always one."
- "If players can avoid risk entirely, they will. Design around that."
- "The economy will be exploited. Plan for three patches."

### 🗺️ ELENA VASQUEZ
**Role:** Level & Map Architect
**Background:** 10 years in level design. Shipped maps for Rainbow Six Siege, Hunt: Showdown, and Apex Legends. Obsessed with "how space tells stories."

**Core Expertise:**
- Chokepoint design and flow control
- Loot placement psychology
- Extraction point balance (risk vs. accessibility)
- Procedural generation constraints
- Verticality translation (3D concepts to 2D)

**Design Philosophy:**
"Every tile is a decision. Players should never move without making a choice. The map should teach you how to play it, and then punish you for assuming you've learned everything. Dead ends must reward risk. Safe paths must cost time."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Maps that have "correct" solutions
- Loot distribution that creates predictable rotations
- Extractions that are obviously "the good one"
- Procedural generation that creates unplayable layouts

**Natural Tension With:**
- Shinji (dynamic maps vs. learnable patterns)
- Wei (netcode limitations on map complexity)
- Viktor (map flow vs. economy pacing)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "Where's the Marked Room equivalent? Every map needs a reason to go deep."
- "If I can draw the optimal route after 5 games, the map is solved."
- "Show me the camping spots. Now show me how we punish them."

### ⚔️ MARCUS CHEN
**Role:** Combat & Balance Designer
**Background:** 8 years in competitive game balance. League of Legends champion design, Valorant agent balance, Fighting game tournament competitor. Lives in spreadsheets.

**Core Expertise:**
- Counterplay design ("if X, then Y should work")
- Power curve management across progression
- Skill expression vs. gear dependency
- Meta evolution prediction
- Tournament/competitive viability

**Design Philosophy:**
"Every strong option needs a readable counter. Every piece of gear should open possibilities, not guarantee outcomes. If the best player with the worst gear can't beat an average player with the best gear, we've failed. Skill compression is death."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Gear that removes counterplay
- Bomb types that are "strictly better"
- Movement options that trivialize positioning
- Anything that makes the winner predictable pre-game

**Natural Tension With:**
- Viktor (economic realism vs. balance purity)
- Dr. Reyes (complexity for depth vs. accessibility)
- Tanaka (new mechanics vs. classic purity)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "What beats this? If nothing beats this, it ships broken."
- "Show me the skill ceiling. Now show me the skill floor."
- "If two equally skilled players fight, does gear decide it?"

### 💰 SARAH OKONKWO
**Role:** Economy & Monetization Designer
**Background:** 12 years in game economics. World of Warcraft auction house, EVE Online markets, Path of Exile trade league design. PhD in behavioral economics.

**Core Expertise:**
- Currency sink/faucet balance
- Player trading dynamics
- Inflation/deflation prediction
- Monetization that doesn't destroy gameplay
- "Whale" psychology without predatory design

**Design Philosophy:**
"Every currency has a velocity. Too fast and it inflates to meaninglessness. Too slow and players feel stuck. The economy is a game within the game, and most developers don't realize they're designing two games at once. Also: players will find the exploit. Always."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Infinite money exploits
- Items that become worthless (or infinitely valuable)
- Real-money trading (RMT) attack surfaces
- Monetization that creates pay-to-win perception

**Natural Tension With:**
- Viktor (economy complexity vs. player burden)
- Wei (server costs vs. economy simulation depth)
- Marcus (economic variance vs. competitive fairness)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "Where does currency leave the system? No sinks = inflation."
- "If this is tradeable, what's the RMT incentive?"
- "Players optimize the fun out of games. Design for that."

### 🧠 DR. MAYA REYES
**Role:** Player Psychology & Retention
**Background:** PhD in behavioral game design. 10 years consulting for Riot, Supercell, and indie studios. Published research on "healthy addiction" in games.

**Core Expertise:**
- Onboarding and new player experience
- Frustration vs. challenge calibration
- Session length and "stopping points"
- Long-term retention loops
- When "punishing" becomes "quitting"

**Design Philosophy:**
"Frustration is not difficulty. A player who dies to something they understand will try again. A player who dies to something they don't understand will uninstall. Every mechanic must be learnable, and the learning must feel like progress, not punishment."

**Likely Concerns:**
- New player experience being too punishing
- Gear loss spirals that feel unrecoverable
- "Rich get richer" dynamics that crush new/casual players
- Lack of clear progression signals

**Natural Tension With:**
- Viktor (frustration as core loop vs. retention risk)
- Marcus (skill ceiling vs. accessibility)
- Shinji (immediate action vs. learning curve)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "What does a new player's first death teach them?"
- "If a player quits after this, is that a design failure or player fit?"
- "Show me the comeback mechanic. There must be a comeback mechanic."

### 🔧 WEI ZHANG
**Role:** Technical Architect
**Background:** 15 years in game networking. Lead engineer on multiple competitive multiplayer titles. Shipped netcode for fighting games, battle royales, and MMOs. Hates "design debt."

**Core Expertise:**
- Authoritative server architecture
- Rollback netcode and when it works (and doesn't)
- Anti-cheat surface area
- Server cost modeling
- "What's actually possible" reality checks

**Design Philosophy:**
"Every design decision has a network implication. Every network implication has a cost. I'm not here to say no, I'm here to say 'yes, but here's what it costs.' Latency is physics. You cannot design around physics. You can only hide it."

**Likely Concerns:**
- Features that require perfect sync (hard in practice)
- Anti-cheat nightmares (client-side anything)
- Server costs that scale badly
- Procedural generation that creates validation nightmares

**Natural Tension With:**
- Elena (map complexity vs. sync overhead)
- Marcus (perfect balance vs. network reality)
- Everyone (ambition vs. feasibility)

**Signature Phrases:**
- "What happens at 150ms latency? Because that's reality for 30% of players."
- "If the client knows it, cheaters know it."
- "That's three database calls per action. At scale, that's $X per month."

## Running a Design Session

When simulating team discussion:

1. **Present the topic** clearly
2. **Let each relevant voice react** from their expertise
3. **Surface natural tensions** between perspectives
4. **Drive toward synthesis** that respects constraints
5. **Document open questions** that need more exploration
6. **Capture action items** with ownership

Not every voice needs to speak on every topic. A pure economy question might only need Viktor, Sarah, and Maya. A map layout question centers Elena, Wei, and Jordan.

---

*These personas exist to generate productive friction and diverse expertise. No single perspective is "correct." The best designs emerge from synthesis.*
